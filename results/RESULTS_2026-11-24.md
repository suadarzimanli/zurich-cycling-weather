# Results of the main question (24 November 2026)

All definitions are those frozen in `docs/PLAN_2026-11-24.md` and `docs/ANALYSIS_DETAILS_2026-11-24.md` before counts and weather were joined. Code: `code/07_results.py`. Full output: `results/results_run_2026-11-24.log`.

## Short answer

Cycling in Zurich has become **somewhat less sensitive to rain, but it is far from weather-proof**.
- **Then:** in 2011–2014 a wet day cut daytime bike traffic by about 33%.
- **Now:** in 2022–2026 the cut is about 29% at the same nine locations.
- **Cold:** sensitivity to cold did not change clearly.
- **Fit with the e-bike explanation:** the pattern does not match what that explanation predicted.

## The three predictions

| Prediction | Result | Supported? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The drop on wet days is smaller in 2022–2026 than in 2011–2014 | −33.4% early, −28.8% late. Change +0.068 log points, 95% interval +0.021 to +0.115. The rain effect shrank by 17% (standard error 5%) | **Yes** |
| 2. The cold and heat effects shrink more than the rain effect | Cold shrank by 13%, which is not more than rain (difference −3 points, interval −28 to +21). Heat: see the note below | **No** |
| 3. The change also shows in weekday rush hours | −32.0% early, −27.9% late. Change +0.058, interval +0.006 to +0.110 | **Yes** |

**Note on heat:** the plan expected heat above 25 °C to reduce cycling. In fact, in 2011–2014 each degree above 25 °C *added* about 1.0% riders (interval 0.1% to 1.8%). In 2022–2026 the effect is about zero (0.1%). The heat effect therefore "shrank" by 86%, but in the direction of hot days losing their pull, not of heat becoming less of a deterrent. This is not the pattern the e-bike explanation predicts. Prediction 2 fails in any case, because it needs both cold and heat.

## Effects by year (all 33 locations)

The main figure is `results/fig_effects_by_year.png` (and `.pdf`).
- **Wet day:** −28% to −37% from 2011 to 2013, about −25% to −30% from 2017 onwards. The exception is 2019 (−34%).
- **Cold:** about 3% to 4% more riders per degree in 2011–2014, about 2% to 3% from 2015 onwards. Single years are noisy.
- **Heat:** close to zero in most years, with wide intervals.

## Checks (prediction 1)

| Check | Change in the rain effect | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|
| Main (9 locations, Fluntern, cleaned) | +0.068 | +0.021 to +0.115 |
| Weather from Affoltern | +0.087 | +0.038 to +0.135 |
| No cleaning rule | +0.071 | +0.023 to +0.119 |
| All 33 locations | +0.104 | +0.059 to +0.149 |
| MeteoSwiss daily rain (6:00 to 6:00 UTC) | +0.028 | −0.011 to +0.067 |

The result holds in three of the four checks. It does not hold with MeteoSwiss's daily rain total. That total covers 7:00 or 8:00 to 7:00 or 8:00 local time on the next day, so it includes night rain and morning rain of the next day. It is a cruder measure: the rain effect itself is only about half as large with it (−21% instead of −33% early), and the change shrinks with it. The plan named daytime rain as the main measure.

With all 33 locations the change is larger (−32.5% to −25.1%). The two periods then compare different sets of locations, so the 9-location result is the cleaner comparison.

## What this can and cannot show

- **What it shows:** the weakening of the rain effect is measured with weather that cyclists do not choose, within the same locations and months.
- **What it cannot show:** why it happened. E-bikes, better bike lanes, rain clothing and new kinds of riders all changed at the same time.
- **The e-bike explanation:** prediction 2 was the test of the e-bike explanation, and it was not supported.
- **Rush hours:** the change also appears in weekday rush hours, so it is not only a change in the mix of trips (more commuting, less leisure).

# Results of the forecasting question (24 November 2026)

All definitions are those frozen in `docs/FORECAST_DETAILS_2026-11-24.md`, including its addendum. The test covers 19 locations and 540 days (1 June 2025 to 22 November 2026), which is 9,594 forecasts, the same for every model. Code: `code/09` to `code/12`. Scores: `results/forecast/scores_all.json`.

## Error compared with "same day last week"

A value of 0.50 means half the error of the simple baseline. All models beat the baseline clearly (Diebold–Mariano p below 0.001).

| Model | With the next day's weather (best case) | Without it (worst case) |
|---|---|---|
| Same day last week | 1.00 (MAE 326 bikes) | 1.00 |
| LightGBM | 0.50 | **0.68** |
| Network without graph (RNN) | 0.54 | 0.75 |
| **Graph neural network (Graph WaveNet)** | **0.41** | 0.71 |

## Does the graph help?

- **Yes.** Graph WaveNet has 24% less error than the same network without the graph when the weather is known (ratio 0.76, t = −11.7). It has 6% less error without the weather (ratio 0.94, t = −4.4). This was the planned graph test.
- **Against LightGBM** (an extra comparison, not planned): the graph network is better when the weather is known (ratio 0.83, t = −7.2). It is slightly worse without the weather (ratio 1.04, t = +2.1, p = 0.04).
- **Seeds:** results differ between the three seeds of the graph network (ratios 0.48, 0.43 and 0.44 in the best case). The reported forecast is their mean, as planned.
- **Caveat:** Graph WaveNet also learns extra links between counters on top of the distance graph. The gain therefore comes from the graph as a whole, not only from distance.

## Weather matters for forecasting

Knowing the next day's weather cuts the error by 26% to 42%, depending on the model. For example, LightGBM goes from 0.68 to 0.50 and Graph WaveNet from 0.71 to 0.41. Real weather forecasts are less accurate than observed weather, so a real system would land between the two columns.

## Drift test (link to the main question)

- **Setup:** LightGBM trained only on 2011–2015, scored on the test period at the 11 locations that have data in both periods.
- **Result:** it under-predicts wet days by 15% and dry days by 8%. The difference is −7.7 percentage points (95% interval −13.0 to −2.0).
- **Reference:** the model trained on recent data shows no such gap (+1.4, interval −3.5 to +7.0).
- **Meaning:** an old model still expects rain to cut traffic as much as it did in 2011–2015. This agrees with the main result.

## Training cost

Each graph network run took 43 to 100 minutes on the CPU, and each network without the graph took 6 to 10 minutes.

# Extra checks after the results (24 November 2026)

These checks were not in the frozen plan. They were added after the results to test two weak points. Code: `code/13` to `code/15`.

## Check A: could the new counters explain the weaker rain effect?

All 9 comparison locations got new counters between the two periods (most in winter 2018–2019). If new counters measured rain days differently, the change could come from the equipment.

- **Swaps:** at 18 locations (8 of the 9 key ones), the rain effect was compared in the last year of the original counter and the first year of the next long-lived counter. Short temporary counters were skipped.
- **Result at the swaps:** the rain effect became *stronger*, not weaker: −0.049 (interval −0.087 to −0.011) for all 18 locations, −0.130 (interval −0.210 to −0.050) for the key locations. This is the opposite direction of the main result.
- **Counters not replaced:** at the 11 locations whose counter was *not* replaced between 2018 and 2019, the rain effect also became stronger from 2018 to 2019, by −0.116 (interval −0.205 to −0.026). So the jump belongs to the year 2019, not to the new counters.
- **Conclusion:** the counter replacements do not explain the weaker rain effect.

## Check B: is the drift result caused by season?

- **Method:** wet and dry days were compared within the same month and the same location.
- **Result:** the old model still under-predicts wet days more than dry days, but by less: −5.7 points instead of −7.7. The 95% interval now just includes zero (−10.0 to +0.5).
- **Reference model:** +3.0 points (interval −1.6 to +8.7).
- **Conclusion:** part of the original drift result came from season. The drift test still points the same way as the main result, but on its own it is not statistically clear. The paper should present it as supporting evidence only.
