# Is cycling in Zurich becoming weather-proof? Research plan (24 November 2026)

This plan is written before any weather effect has been estimated. We only checked the coverage and quality of the data and built separate tables for counts, weather and holidays (scripts `01` to `05`). Count data and weather data have not been joined.

## 1. Questions

**Main question.** Has the effect of rain, cold and heat on bicycle traffic in Zurich changed between 2011 and 2026?

**Second question.** How well can the daily bicycle traffic at each counter be forecast one day ahead? Does a model that uses the network of counters (a graph neural network) do better than simple methods?

## 2. Why it matters

- **E-Bike City.** ETH's E-Bike City project asks what happens if Zurich gives half of its road space to bikes. A study of 435 reader comments on this idea found weather among the prominent themes (Elliot, Wicki, Egan and Axhausen, 2024). If cycling has become less sensitive to weather, that concern is weaker. If it has not, plans for a cycling city must be built around wet and cold days.
- **E-bikes.** E-bikes grew from about 23% of new bikes sold in Switzerland in 2016 to about 45% in 2024. Studies of single years suggest that e-bike riders are less put off by cold and heat than other cyclists, but not by rain.
- **A gap in the research.** Weather and cycling is well studied, but almost always over one to three years. We found no study that measures how the weather effect changes over 15 years. Zurich's counters started in 2009, which makes this possible.

## 3. Data (all open)

| Data | Source | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Bicycle counts, every 15 minutes, both directions | City of Zurich open data (CC0), downloaded 24 November 2026 | December 2009 to 22 November 2026. 85 counter installations at 33 locations |
| Counter locations and correction factors | City of Zurich geodata | All locations, with start and end dates |
| Weather, 10-minute values | MeteoSwiss, station Zürich/Fluntern (Zürich/Affoltern as a check) | 2010 to 2026, 99.98% complete |
| School holidays | City of Zurich open data (CC0) | From 2018. Earlier years are rebuilt from the city's calendar-week rule. The rule matches the official file for 2018 to 2026 and a published list for 2011 to 2018 exactly |
| Public holidays and the two local half-days (Sechseläuten, Knabenschiessen) | Fixed dates and the Easter rule for the Canton of Zurich, checked against the official file | Whole period |

**What the data checks showed:**
- Only one location has data in 2010, so the study starts in 2011. Eleven locations have nearly complete years from 2011 to 2014, and 14 to 23 locations from 2015 onwards.
- Most devices were replaced around the turn of 2018 to 2019. A replaced device gets a new ID, but it stays at the same location.
- The 2026 file and the combined file agree exactly where they overlap. The duplicate time stamps all fall at night on clock-change days, outside the hours we use.
- MeteoSwiss time stamps are in UTC and mark the end of each interval. MeteoSwiss computed hourly values on a different schedule before 2018. To avoid this break, all weather variables are built from the 10-minute values.

## 4. Design for the main question

- **Unit:** one counter location on one day. A location keeps its identity when its device is replaced. Each device period gets its own baseline level, because a new device can count differently.
- **Outcome:** the number of bikes in both directions between 6:00 and 20:00 local time. Days with any missing 15-minute value in that window are dropped.
- **Cleaning rule, fixed now:** a day is dropped if it has zero bikes, if it has less than 5% or more than 5 times the median of the same location in the same month, or if its total is identical on three or more days in a row. These are counter faults or special events. All results are also shown without this rule.
- **Weather:** rain between 6:00 and 20:00 local time, the highest temperature of the day, and minutes of sunshine between 6:00 and 20:00, all from Fluntern.
- **Model:** a count regression (Poisson). It compares each location with itself within the same month of the same year, with separate levels for each weekday at each location, for public holidays, for school holidays and for the two local half-days. This removes the season, long-term growth, construction work and device changes. The weather effect then comes only from day-to-day changes in weather, which cyclists do not choose.
- **Weather terms:**
  - Rain in three classes: dry (under 0.1 mm), light (0.1 to 2 mm) and wet (2 mm or more).
  - Temperature as a line that may bend at 5, 15 and 25 °C. The **cold effect** is the change per degree below 15 °C. The **heat effect** is the change per degree above 25 °C.
- **Change over time:** the weather effects are estimated separately for each year from 2011 to 2026. The main figure shows the rain, cold and heat effects year by year.
- **Uncertainty:** standard errors that allow days in the same week to be related (clustered by week).

## 5. Predictions fixed before looking

1. **Rain:** the drop in traffic on a wet day, compared with a dry day, is smaller in 2022–2026 than in 2011–2014.
2. **Cold and heat compared with rain:** the cold effect and the heat effect shrink more than the rain effect. This is the pattern we would expect if the spread of e-bikes is behind the change.
3. **Rush hours:** the change also shows in weekday rush hours (7:00 to 9:00 and 16:00 to 19:00), not only at weekends. This checks that the change is not only a shift in who rides.

**Rules for the tests:**
- The period comparison uses the 9 locations that have at least two calendar years with 300 or more complete days in both periods (2011–2014 and 2022–2025): Andreasstrasse, Binzmühlestrasse, Hofwiesenstrasse, Lux-Guyer-Weg, Mühlebachstrasse, Mythenquai, Scheuchzerstrasse, Schulstrasse and Sihlpromenade. All locations together are reported as a check.
- With 9 locations, small changes may not be detectable. The year-by-year figure for all locations is reported in any case.
- 2020 and 2021 (the pandemic) are shown in the figure but left out of the period comparison.
- The early period has 153 days of 25 °C or more (30 of them at 30 °C or more). If the heat effect is too imprecise, the heat result is reported as exploratory.
- All results are reported, including results that do not support the predictions.

**Checks:** weather from Affoltern instead of Fluntern, no cleaning rule, MeteoSwiss daily rain instead of daytime rain, and the period comparison with all locations.

**What this can and cannot show:** the change in the weather effect over time can be measured cleanly. The reason for the change cannot be proven, because e-bikes, new bike lanes and new kinds of riders all grew at the same time. Predictions 2 and 3 test whether the pattern fits the e-bike explanation.

## 6. Design for the second question (forecasting)

- **Task:** forecast the next day's bike count (6:00 to 20:00) at each location.
- **Data split:** models are trained on data up to 31 May 2025 and tested from 1 June 2025 to 22 November 2026.
- **Models:**
  1. the same weekday one week earlier (simple baseline)
  2. a tree model (LightGBM) with the calendar, recent counts and weather
  3. a graph neural network that links counters by distance, built with the Torch Spatiotemporal library
- **Weather input:** two versions. One uses the observed weather of the target day, which is the best possible case. The other uses no weather for the target day, which is the worst case. Real weather forecasts fall between the two.
- **Link to the main question:** does a model trained only on 2011–2015 make larger errors on wet and cold days today? If the weather effect has changed, old models should be wrong in a predictable way.
- **Scoring:** mean absolute error compared with the simple baseline, with a Diebold–Mariano test, as in the IEEE and JPR work.

## 7. Outputs

- A working paper of about 8 pages with one main figure (weather effects by year).
- Code on GitHub with a Zenodo DOI. The raw data are not copied into the repository. A script downloads them from the official sources.

## 8. Steps

1. Freeze this plan before any weather effect is estimated. Its checksum is recorded in `docs/FROZEN_SHA256.txt`. It is not registered publicly.
2. Build the dataset: counts, weather and holidays (2 to 3 days).
3. Main question: model and main figure (about one week).
4. Forecasting: baselines and LightGBM first, then install PyTorch and add the graph neural network (about one week).
5. Write the working paper, then publish the code on GitHub and Zenodo (about one week).
